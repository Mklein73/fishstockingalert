Mentored Youth Trout Day in Pennsylvania: A Complete Guide

One Saturday a year, kids get first crack at stocked trout waters a week before a few hundred thousand adults arrive.

Fish Stocking Alert • Planning • 9 min read

Mentored Youth Trout Day is the one Saturday a year when kids get first crack at Pennsylvania's stocked trout waters, a week before the statewide opener and before a few hundred thousand adults arrive. The water is full, the fish have never seen a lure, and the crowd is families.

The rules are specific and the permitting trips people up every year. Here is exactly how the day works.

What the Day Is

PFBC runs a single statewide Mentored Youth Trout Day, held on a Saturday roughly one week before the opening day of trout season. Fishing hours run from 8 a.m. to 7:30 p.m.

The 2026 day was March 28, ahead of the April 4 opener. The 2025 day was March 29, ahead of the April 5 opener. PFBC typically confirms the following year's date in late fall or with the January stocking schedule release.

The waters included are the Stocked Trout Waters statewide, the same ones listed for the opening day of trout season. Other waters may be added. Special regulation areas are not included in the program. That means Delayed Harvest sections, catch and release sections, and similar waters are outside the day.

It is unlawful to fish waters designated as part of the Mentored Youth Fishing Day Program except when participating in the program, and unlawful to fish those waters after 7:30 p.m. on the day.

Who Can Fish and What Everyone Needs

This is where most of the confusion lives. Both the youth and the adult need something, and the requirements are not the same.

The Youth Angler

A youth is anyone under 16. To participate, the youth needs one of two things from the Commission:

  • A Mentored Youth Permit, which is free.
  • A Voluntary Youth Fishing License, which costs $2.97. That breaks down as a $1 cost, a $1 issuing agent fee, and a $0.97 PALS transaction fee.

Either one qualifies the youth to fish the day. The Voluntary Youth License supports PFBC youth programs all year, which is why the Commission encourages it over the free permit.

The youth must have their own HuntFishPA account. You cannot buy the permit under a parent's account.

The Adult Mentor

An adult 16 or older must possess a valid Pennsylvania fishing license and a current trout permit, and must be accompanied by a youth who holds the Mentored Youth Permit or Voluntary Youth License.

The adult needs the trout permit even though the adult cannot keep a trout. That catches people.

The Ratio Rule

One licensed angler may mentor multiple youth. There may be no more than one licensed angler per actively fishing mentored youth. So one adult can take three kids, but three adults cannot fish behind one kid.

The Harvest Rules

These are the rules that matter most on the water.

  • Only the youth may possess trout. A youth with a current Voluntary Youth Fishing License or Mentored Youth Permit may possess a total of two trout, combined species, with a seven inch minimum length.
  • Adults are prohibited from possessing trout. Full stop. The adult fishes, and any trout the adult catches goes back.
  • All other Commonwealth inland regulations apply.

Two fish per kid. That is the day. Plan the cooler accordingly.

How to Get the Permit

Everything runs through HuntFishPA or a licensing agent.

New Customers

Create an account for the youth at HuntFishPA. You will need the youth's date of birth, Social Security number, street address and zip code, and an email address to link to the account. Then confirm Pennsylvania residency and create a username.

From the account homepage, click Buy Licenses/Permits, go to the Fish tab, and search for either the Voluntary Youth Annual Fishing License at $2.97 or the Mentored Youth Fishing Permit Annual, which is free. Add to cart and complete checkout.

On the order receipt page, download the receipt and license documents. Page 2 of the receipt is the youth's license or permit. Keep a printed or digital copy on hand for the day.

Returning Customers

Log in to the youth's existing account. You will need the username and password, or the customer number, date of birth, zip code, and associated email. Same purchase steps from there.

If you cannot get into the account, use the Account Lookup and Forgot Customer Number tools on the HuntFishPA main page, or call HuntFishPA support at 1-800-838-4431. The nine digit customer number is also printed on any previous fishing license.

Do this in February. Do not do it Friday night.

Guidance for Mentors

PFBC publishes specific expectations for how the day is supposed to work, and they are worth reading as more than legal boilerplate.

  • The youth should be capable of fishing on their own, with limited assistance from the mentor.
  • The mentor should be within arm's reach, roughly six feet, of the actively fishing mentored youth.
  • PFBC discourages youth organizations from using the day to get a large group of kids fishing with minimal adult supervision.

The spirit of it is straightforward. The kid fishes. You help. This is not a day for an adult to run four rods with a child standing nearby.

Picking a Spot

The whole appeal of the day is that the water has been stocked and nobody has fished it. Preseason stocking begins around the third week of February, so by late March most stocked sections are loaded.

Three things to weigh:

Bank access over wading. A stream with a mowed bank, a park path, or a lake shoreline beats a technical wade. Kids fall in. Pick water where that is an inconvenience rather than an emergency.

Stocked lakes are underrated. Casting is easier, there is no current to manage, and a slip float with dough bait requires no skill to fish correctly. A lake with a dock or a level shoreline is the highest-success option for a first-timer.

Check what was stocked. Sections that carry golden rainbow allocations are a good target, because a bright yellow trout is a memory. Our golden rainbow guide covers where they go, and note that Mentored Youth waters often receive them.

Remember that the planned allocation for a section is an annual figure across all its stockings, and the scheduled date is a separate fact. All stockings are subject to change without notice. Our guide to reading the Pennsylvania trout stocking schedule covers how to work that out for a given section.

If the day goes well, the statewide opener follows a week later. Our opening day guide covers what changes, and our license and permit guide covers what the youth will need once they turn 16.

Gear for the Day

Keep it simple. Complexity is what makes kids quit.

  • Rod. A five foot to five and a half foot light spinning combo, pre-spooled with 4 or 6 pound line. Do not hand a kid a seven foot rod.
  • Rig. Slip sinker, a small barrel swivel, an 18 inch leader, and a size 12 or 14 treble with floating dough bait. It fishes itself.
  • Bait. Chartreuse and rainbow dough baits, plus a jar of salmon eggs as a backup. Bring more than you need, because it will end up on the ground.
  • Extras. A rod holder, a bite alarm or a bell, a net, hemostats for hook removal, and a folding chair.
  • Comfort. Late March in Pennsylvania is cold. Layers, a hat, gloves, extra socks, hand warmers, and snacks. A cold, bored kid leaves in an hour.

Bring a camera. That is the actual point of the day.

Managing the Day

Start at 8 a.m. if you can, because the first two hours produce the most fish. Do not plan to stay until 7:30. Two to three hours is a good day for most kids, and leaving while they still want to fish is how you get invited back.

Let them reel. Let them net. Let them hold the fish for the photo, wet hands and all. Adults who take over the rod at the strike are the reason kids lose interest.

If nothing is biting after 45 minutes, move. Kids do not have the patience to grind out a slow spot, and they should not have to.

Teaching the Basics Before the Day

The kids who have the best Mentored Youth Day are the ones who practiced casting in a yard the week before. Twenty minutes with a practice plug on grass removes most of the frustration that ends a day early.

Three things worth practicing:

  • The cast. A short underhand lob is easier than an overhead cast and it works fine at the twenty foot range most bank fishing requires.
  • The bail. Opening and closing the bail on a spinning reel is the single most common source of tangles. Make it automatic.
  • Setting the hook. Lift the rod, do not yank. Kids either do nothing or rip the bait fifteen feet in the air.

Skip knots. Tie everything yourself, and bring pre-rigged spares so a tangle takes thirty seconds to solve instead of ten minutes.

What to Do When the Fishing Is Slow

Not every spot produces, and a kid who is not catching gets bored faster than an adult who is not catching.

Move after 45 minutes with no action. Trout stack in specific holes on a stocked stream, and the difference between the right pool and the wrong one is total.

Switch targets rather than switching baits. Suckers, fallfish, and panfish are usually catchable when trout are not, and a kid does not care what species is on the end of the line. A steady bite on chubs beats a slow morning waiting for a trout.

Bring something else to do. A net for looking at crayfish and macroinvertebrates under rocks holds attention as well as fishing does and teaches more about why trout live where they live.

Handling and Cleaning Fish With Kids

Two trout per youth is a small enough limit to make cleaning a teachable moment rather than a chore.

Let them do part of it if they want to and none of it if they do not. Neither reaction is a problem. Show them where the fish came from, why it has a fin clip if it has one, and what it was eating when you open the stomach.

For fish going back, wet hands, keep the fish in the net, unhook it in the water, and let the kid watch it swim off. That moment lands as well as keeping one does, and it teaches the habit early.

Bring a small cooler with ice for anything you keep. A trout carried around on a stringer in the sun for four hours is not going to taste good, and a bad first fish dinner does not help the cause.

Where This Fits in the Season

Mentored Youth Trout Day is the opening act. The statewide opener follows a week later, in-season stockings run through spring, and fall stocking picks up in October.

A kid who has one good Saturday in March is a kid who will ask to go again in April. That is the actual return on the $2.97 license, and it is the reason PFBC runs the program at all.

Quick Reference

  • When. One statewide Saturday about a week before opening day, 8 a.m. to 7:30 p.m.
  • Recent dates. March 29 in 2025, March 28 in 2026. Confirm the next year's date with PFBC.
  • Youth needs. Free Mentored Youth Permit or $2.97 Voluntary Youth Fishing License, in the youth's own HuntFishPA account.
  • Adult needs. Valid fishing license plus current trout permit.
  • Youth limit. Two trout combined species, seven inch minimum.
  • Adult limit. Zero. Adults may not possess trout.
  • Ratio. One adult may mentor multiple youth. No more than one licensed adult per actively fishing youth.
  • Excluded water. Special regulation areas are not part of the program.
