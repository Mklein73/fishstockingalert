Stocked Trout Waters Open to Year-Round Fishing in Pennsylvania

The waters you can legally fish during the late-winter closure, and the three-tier limit structure that governs them.

Fish Stocking Alert • Regulations • 9 min read

Pennsylvania closes its stocked trout waters to all fishing from mid-February until 8 a.m. on the opening day of trout season. That is roughly six weeks when trucks are dumping fish into water you are not allowed to touch. The Stocked Trout Waters Open to Year-Round Fishing program is the legal way around it.

These waters carry stocked trout, receive stockings on the normal schedule, and stay open every day of the year. Most anglers do not know which ones they are, which is exactly why they are worth knowing.

What the Program Is

Under 58 Pa. Code section 65.19, the PFBC Executive Director, with Commission approval, may designate specific waters as stocked trout waters open to year-round fishing. Designation takes effect when notice is published in the Pennsylvania Bulletin.

The program is mostly lakes and ponds, with a smaller number of stream sections. Waters get added and removed. In one recent example, Blue Hole Creek Section 02 in Somerset County was removed from the program after the Department of Environmental Protection documented high concentrations of cadmium and lead in part of the stream, and PFBC discontinued stocking there.

The Three-Season Limit Structure

These waters run under their own season and creel structure, and it changes three times a year.

Regular Season

From 8 a.m. on the opening day of trout season through midnight on Labor Day. Seven inch minimum size, five trout per day combined species.

Extended Season

From 12:01 a.m. the day after Labor Day to midnight the third Monday in February of the following year. Seven inch minimum, three trout per day combined species. For the current cycle that window runs from September 8, 2026 through February 15, 2027.

Late Winter, No Harvest

From 12:01 a.m. the third Monday in February to 8 a.m. on the opening day of the regular trout season, these waters are catch and immediate release only. You can fish. You cannot keep anything.

That last window is the point of the program. While every other stocked trout water in the state is closed, you can legally fish these, and preseason stocking is happening at the same time.

The Trout Permit Rule Catches People

The permit requirement is not uniform across the program, and the distinction is easy to get wrong.

On rivers and streams designated as stocked trout waters open to year-round fishing, a current trout permit is required to fish at all. Not just to keep trout. To fish.

On lakes and ponds in the program, a trout permit is not required unless you take, kill, or possess a trout or salmon while fishing. So you can fish a year-round lake for panfish or bass without a trout permit, but the moment a trout goes in your bucket you need one.

Which Waters Are in the Program

The list runs county by county in the PFBC regulations summary. A partial picture of what is out there in the eastern half of the state:

Southeast Region

  • Berks. Kaercher Creek Dam. Tulpehocken Creek from the covered bridge at T-921 downstream to the mouth.
  • Bucks. Lake Luxembourg.
  • Lancaster. Muddy Run Recreation Lake.
  • Lehigh. Leaser Lake.
  • Schuylkill. Sweet Arrow Lake, Tuscarora Lake.

Northeast Region

  • Luzerne. Frances Slocum Lake, Harveys Lake, Lake Irena, Lake Took A While, Lily Lake, Moon Lake, Sylvan Lake.
  • Lackawanna. Lackawanna Lake.
  • Carbon and Luzerne. Francis E. Walter Reservoir. The Lehigh River from the F. E. Walter Reservoir outlet downstream to Sandy Run, and a separate stretch from Glen Onoko downriver to Mauch Chunk Creek.
  • Columbia. Briar Creek Lake.
  • Monroe. Hidden Lake.
  • Pike. Lily Pond, Little Mud Pond.
  • Sullivan. Hunters Lake.
  • Susquehanna. Quaker Lake.
  • Wayne. Duck Harbor Pond, Upper Woods Pond.
  • Wyoming. Lake Winola. Mehoopany Creek from Henry Lott Brook to the North Fork confluence.
  • Bradford. Cooks Pond, Mountain Lake, Sugar Run from T-420 to the mouth.

The western and central regions carry their own lists. Because designations change, verify your specific water in the current PFBC regulations summary before you plan a February trip.

The Two Windows Worth Planning Around

Late February Through Opening Day

This is the reason to learn this list. Preseason stocking begins around the third week of February. Regular stocked waters close at the same time. Year-round waters do not.

So a lake like Leaser or Sweet Arrow can take a truckload of trout in early March and be legally fishable that afternoon, catch and release. You are fishing fresh, naive trout with no crowd and no closed season. Bring a camera instead of a stringer.

December Through Ice-Up

Year-round lakes get a lot of the November and December stockings that PFBC schedules specifically to set up ice fishing. That means fish are in place and settled before hardwater. When the ice comes in January, you are drilling over a population that has been there for weeks.

How to Fish Them

Most of these waters are lakes, so lake tactics apply. The standard setup is a slip sinker rig with an 18 to 24 inch fluorocarbon leader and floating dough bait in chartreuse or rainbow. Cast toward the first depth break rather than straight out from the bank.

For anglers who prefer to move, small spoons in 1/8 ounce and countdown minnow baits in the two inch range fished along points and channel edges select for better fish. In the no-harvest February window, switch to single barbless hooks and skip the treble-hooked hardware. You are releasing everything anyway, and unhooking goes faster.

On the stream sections, particularly the Lehigh River stretches, winter means slow deep water. Marabou jigs under a float and small nymphs dead drifted through the deepest holding water. The Lehigh runs cold and big, so wade carefully and check flows before you commit to the drive.

How This Differs From Delayed Harvest Water

Both programs stay open year-round, which is where the confusion starts.

Year-round waters allow harvest for most of the year, five fish in the regular season and three in the extended season, and they permit any legal tackle including bait. The no-harvest period is only the few weeks between the third Monday in February and opening day.

Delayed Harvest water flips that. Harvest is allowed only from June 15 through Labor Day, and tackle is restricted to artificial lures, flies, and streamers all year. Our guide to Pennsylvania delayed harvest waters covers the details.

If you want to keep fish and use bait in March, you want a year-round water. If you want a dense population of educated carryover trout, you want DHALO.

Why These Designations Exist

The program solves a specific problem. Pennsylvania closes its stocked trout waters in late winter to protect fish that have just been stocked and to make opening day a genuine event. That closure costs anglers six weeks of fishable water at a time of year when nothing else is going on.

Designating a subset of waters as open year-round preserves the opening day tradition on most of the state while giving anglers somewhere legal to go. The catch and immediate release rule during the closure window means the fish stocked for opening day are still there on opening day.

Waters get selected and removed based on angler demand, access, and biological considerations. The Blue Hole Creek removal is a good illustration of how the second part works. When the Department of Environmental Protection documented high cadmium and lead concentrations, PFBC discontinued stocking and pulled the section from the program rather than leave anglers fishing water with a health concern.

Ice Fishing These Waters

A large share of the year-round list is lakes and ponds, and a large share of PFBC's November and December stockings go into lakes specifically to set up ice fishing. Those two facts overlap more than coincidentally.

Waters like Frances Slocum Lake, Moon Lake, Lily Lake, Briar Creek Lake, Lackawanna Lake, and Leaser Lake are all in the year-round program and all fish well through the ice when conditions allow.

The advantage of a year-round designation for ice anglers is that the season never closes underneath you. A February thaw that pushes into the closure window does not shut these waters down the way it would on a standard stocked lake.

Standard ice safety applies and matters more than the regulations. No ice is guaranteed safe, Pennsylvania winters swing hard, and thickness varies across a single lake. Check as you go and carry picks.

A Note on the Stream Sections

The stream sections in the program deserve separate treatment because the trout permit rule is stricter there and because they fish differently than the lakes.

The Lehigh River stretches in Carbon and Luzerne counties are the most substantial moving water in the program. The section below the Francis E. Walter Reservoir outlet is tailwater, which means release schedules control it. A whitewater release turns a wadeable river into an unwadeable one in a matter of hours, so check the schedule before you plan a day rather than after you arrive.

Tulpehocken Creek below the T-921 covered bridge is the other notable one. Above the covered bridge the water is Keystone Select and Delayed Harvest. Below it, the year-round rules apply. That is a single creek with two entirely different regulation sets separated by one bridge, and it is worth knowing which side you are standing on.

Sugar Run in Bradford County and Mehoopany Creek in Wyoming County round out the eastern stream sections. All of these require a trout permit to fish, harvest or not.

Quick Reference

  • Season. Open every day of the year.
  • Regular season. Opening day through Labor Day. Five trout, seven inch minimum.
  • Extended season. Day after Labor Day to the third Monday in February. Three trout, seven inch minimum.
  • Late winter. Third Monday in February to opening day. Catch and immediate release only.
  • Trout permit. Required to fish streams in the program. Required on lakes only if you keep a trout or salmon.
  • Tackle. Any legal tackle including bait, unless the water carries an additional special regulation.
  • Best window. Late February through opening day, when everything else is closed.
