Central Pennsylvania Stocked Trout Streams

The region is famous for wild limestone water, which hides the fact that it holds a large stocked trout program too.

Fish Stocking Alert • Location Guide • 10 min read

Central Pennsylvania has a reputation problem for stocked trout anglers. The region is famous for wild limestone water, and that fame crowds out the fact that PFBC stocks a large number of streams and lakes across Centre, Clinton, Huntingdon, Mifflin, Union, Snyder, Clearfield, Blair, and the surrounding counties.

This guide covers the stocked water. It draws a hard line against the wild trout streams, because fishing them with stocked-trout expectations is the fastest way to have a bad day in this part of the state.

The Line Between Stocked and Wild

Central Pennsylvania holds some of the best wild trout water in the eastern United States. Spring Creek in Centre County is the clearest example. It is a wild brown trout fishery, managed under catch and release regulations, and it is not part of the adult trout stocking program. Upper Penns Creek, Big Fishing Creek, and several others fall into similar categories with their own regulations.

Those waters are worth fishing. They are not what this guide is about, and treating them as stocked water leads to two problems. You show up expecting fresh hatchery fish that are not there, and you risk violating regulations that differ substantially from standard stocked trout rules.

Check the designation before you fish. A stream can carry wild trout in one section and stocked trout in another, and Bald Eagle Creek is a good example of exactly that.

Centre County

Bald Eagle Creek

Bald Eagle carries several distinct management sections, which makes it the most useful case study in the region.

Section 03, within the Soaring Eagle Wetland in Huston Township north of Port Matilda, is a Keystone Select Stocked Trout Water and the only one of the 24 in the program that permits all tackle types. It runs under a Miscellaneous Special Regulation identical to Delayed Harvest Artificial Lures Only, except bait is allowed. Year-round angling, harvest of up to three trout at nine inches minimum from June 15 through Labor Day, and no harvest the rest of the year. The section has accessible parking and a trail to a fishing platform.

Under the Keystone Select program, large trout measuring 14 to 20 inches go in at 175 to 225 per mile during the spring stocking periods. This particular section typically receives two spring stockings. Our Keystone Select guide covers the program.

Bald Eagle is also stocked in other sections, including from the Bell Hollow Road bridge down to the inlet of Sayers Lake near the Boggs and Howard township line, and separately below Milesburg. Different sections, different rules.

The Rest of Centre County

Fourteen stocked trout streams and two stocked impoundments run through Centre County. Poe Lake in Poe Valley State Park and Cold Stream Dam near Philipsburg are the two lakes. Between the stocked streams, the Keystone Select section, and the wild water, Centre County has more trout fishing per square mile than anywhere else in the state.

Clearfield, Cambria, and the Western Edge

  • Chest Creek is a Keystone Select stream, added in the 2017 program expansion. It runs through Cambria and Clearfield counties.
  • Little Clearfield Creek is stocked from the Watts Creek and Gazzam Run confluence downstream past the T-584 bridge.
  • Little Muddy Run from the SR 253 bridge downstream.

Elk, Potter, and the Northern Tier

  • First Fork Sinnemahoning Creek is one of the original eight Keystone Select streams. Remote, big, and worth the drive if you want big fish without a crowd.
  • East Branch Clarion River near Glen Hazel in Elk County. A 1.2 mile section extending from 100 meters below East Branch Lake down to the State Game Lands boundary was added to the fall stocking program for 2026, which makes it a specific October target.
  • East Branch Millstone Creek above the Loleta Road bridge.
  • Freeman Run in Potter County appears in the 2026 stocking program.
  • Kinzua Creek in McKean County is a Keystone Select stream from the 2017 expansion.

Lycoming and Sullivan Counties

Loyalsock Creek is an original Keystone Select stream. The section runs 2.8 miles, from about a mile above the Sullivan and Lycoming county line downstream to an unnamed tributary roughly 0.9 mile above Plunketts Creek. Loyalsock is also stocked outside that section, from 0.25 mile above Rock Run Road to SR 973.

Loyalsock is one of the prettiest trout streams in the state and it fishes well from April into June. It runs large and pushy after rain, so check flows.

Sullivan County also holds Elk Creek, Hoagland Branch, Little Loyalsock Creek, Muncy Creek, and Mill Creek at Hillsgrove. Hunters Lake is in the year-round fishing program.

Dauphin, Adams, Franklin, and the Southcentral Belt

  • Wiconisco Creek in Dauphin County is an original Keystone Select stream and the closest one to Harrisburg.
  • Big Cove Creek in Fulton County came in with the 2017 expansion.
  • Conewago Creek from the SR 234 bridge at Arendtsville to Harrisburg Road.
  • Bermudian Creek in two stocked stretches in Adams County.
  • Conococheague Creek from the former dam at Birch Run Reservoir to the Boyers Mill Road bridge.
  • East Branch Antietam Creek from Waynesboro Reservoir to Vineyard Run.
  • Carbaugh Run in Franklin and Adams counties.
  • Clear Creek in Bedford County.

This belt runs on limestone geology, which means several of these streams stay cooler and clearer than freestone water at the same elevation. That is an advantage in June and a challenge in terms of how spooky the fish get.

Timing

Mid-February to opening day. Standard stocked waters close. Delayed Harvest sections and Keystone Select sections stay open, which in this region includes Bald Eagle Section 03, Chest Creek, First Fork Sinnemahoning, Loyalsock, Wiconisco, Big Cove, and Kinzua. Zero harvest, but the water is legal.

April and May. Peak. Preseason and in-season stockings load the region and the hatches on the limestone-influenced water start in earnest.

June. The best month in central Pennsylvania. In-season stockings continue, the crowd has thinned, and water temperatures on most of the region's streams are still in range.

July and August. Freestone streams warm up. Move to elevation on the northern tier, or fish the limestone-influenced water at first light.

October through mid-December. Fall stocking. Sixteen of the 24 Keystone Select sections receive fall fish, and several central Pennsylvania lakes get November and December stockings that set up ice fishing. Our fall stocking guide covers the details.

What to Throw

Freestone Water

In the first 48 hours after a stocking where bait is legal, chartreuse or rainbow dough bait on a size 12 hook with split shot, or single salmon eggs on the bottom seam. After the first week, size 1 and 2 inline spinners with gold blades.

Limestone-Influenced Water

Clearer water, spookier fish, and more consistent insect activity. Downsize to size 0 and 1 spinners on 4 pound fluorocarbon. On fly gear, a four weight with size 16 to 20 beadhead nymphs, and sulphurs in May and June.

Keystone Select and Delayed Harvest Sections

Artificial lures, flies, and streamers only, except on Bald Eagle Section 03 where all tackle types are permitted. Marabou jigs at 1/32 and 1/16 ounce in olive, black, and white under a small float. Size 1 inline spinners. Two inch countdown minnow baits. On fly gear, beadhead pheasant tails in size 14 to 18, sucker spawn, and olive woolly buggers in size 8 and 10 for carryover fish.

Using the Data

Central Pennsylvania has enough stocked sections that picking one at random is a waste of a drive. Two habits help.

Sort by fish per mile, not total count. A section with 2,400 planned trout across ten miles fishes thinner than one with 800 across two miles.

Keep count and date separate. The planned allocation is an annual figure for the section across all stockings. The scheduled date is when a truck is planned to arrive. All stockings are subject to change without notice, and dates for a small share of records are week-of approximations rather than exact.

Lakes and Impoundments

Central Pennsylvania is stream country, but the lakes matter when flows are wrong or when you want ice.

  • Poe Lake in Poe Valley State Park and Cold Stream Dam near Philipsburg, both in Centre County.
  • Colyer Lake, a PFBC-owned lake in Centre County.
  • Rose Valley Lake and Beechwood Lake, Lycoming and Tioga counties.
  • Lake Nessmuk, Tioga County.
  • Walker Lake, Snyder County.
  • Opossum Lake, Cumberland County.
  • Kyle Lake and Cloe Lake, Jefferson County.
  • Duman Lake, Cambria County.

Several of these receive November and December stockings timed for ice fishing. Our winter guide covers working them once ice forms.

Hatch Timing in the Region

Central Pennsylvania has the most reliable insect activity in the state, and even on stocked water it changes what produces.

Late March into April. Blue winged olives and early black stoneflies. Small dark nymphs under an indicator.

Mid-April into May. Grannom caddis on many of the limestone-influenced streams, often in dense numbers. Green caddis pupa patterns in size 14 to 16.

May into June. Sulphurs, the defining hatch of the region. Size 16 and 18, evening emergence, and the one time of year when stocked trout in central Pennsylvania feed selectively enough that fly selection matters.

Summer. Terrestrials. Ants, beetles, and hoppers in size 12 to 16 along grassy banks.

Fall. Blue winged olives again, plus streamer fishing for browns as water cools.

None of this is required on freshly stocked fish, which will eat a spinner or a dough bait on day one. It matters from the second week onward, and it matters a lot on the sections that hold carryover trout.

Water Sources and Summer Temperature

Central Pennsylvania streams fall into three groups, and knowing which one you are on tells you when it fishes.

Freestone streams run on surface runoff. They come up fast after rain, run clear and low in summer, and warm past trout tolerance by mid-July at lower elevations. Fish them in spring and fall.

Limestone-influenced streams get a large share of their flow from underground springs at roughly 50 degrees year round. They stay cooler in summer, warmer in winter, and clearer overall. They fish through more of the year and the fish are spookier.

Tailwaters below bottom-release dams get whatever temperature the dam gives them. Predictable and cold, with flows tied to release schedules rather than rainfall.

A stocked section on a limestone-influenced stream at moderate elevation is the best carryover water in the region, which is where the biggest holdover trout come from.

Quick Reference

  • Best big fish. Bald Eagle Creek Section 03, Loyalsock Creek, First Fork Sinnemahoning, Chest Creek, Wiconisco Creek, Big Cove Creek, Kinzua Creek. All Keystone Select.
  • Only all-tackle Keystone Select in the state. Bald Eagle Creek Section 03, Centre County.
  • Best scenery. Loyalsock Creek, Sullivan and Lycoming counties.
  • Most trout per square mile. Centre County, with 14 stocked streams plus Poe Lake and Cold Stream Dam.
  • Do not confuse. Spring Creek and similar wild trout waters are not part of the stocking program and carry different regulations.
