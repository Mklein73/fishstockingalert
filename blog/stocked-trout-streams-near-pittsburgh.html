Best Stocked Trout Streams Near Pittsburgh

Allegheny County has stocked trout inside the county line, and the Laurel Highlands sit inside a 90 minute drive.

Fish Stocking Alert • Location Guide • 10 min read

Pittsburgh sits closer to good stocked trout water than Philadelphia does. Allegheny County alone carries multiple stocked streams and a Delayed Harvest section, and the mountain water in Somerset, Fayette, and Westmoreland counties is inside a 90 minute drive.

This guide works outward from the city, with notes on the special regulation sections and the seasons each area actually fishes best.

Allegheny County

You do not have to leave the county to fish stocked trout.

North Park Lake and Pine Creek

North Park in the northern suburbs is the most accessible stocked trout fishing in the metro area. Pine Creek is stocked from North Park Lake downstream, and it carries a Delayed Harvest section. That combination of lake and stream in one park, twenty minutes from downtown, makes it the default answer for a weekday evening.

Pressure is heavy in April. It drops off completely by June, and the Delayed Harvest section holds fish year-round because harvest is closed from the day after Labor Day until June 15.

Deer Creek

Stocked from above the West Branch Deer Creek confluence downstream to the bend below the Rich Hill Road bridge. Good size for wading, decent access, and less traffic than North Park.

Big Sewickley Creek

Stocked from the confluence of Big and East Branch Sewickley Creek down to the Big Sewickley Creek Road bridge. West of the city, running toward the Ohio River.

Bull Creek

The stocking limits on Bull Creek were revised for the 2026 season, and the water now extends 6.23 miles from the Thompson Road bridge in Millerstown downstream to the West 4th Avenue bridge in Tarentum. The revision was made to add warm-water and cool-water opportunity in the lower section, so the trout emphasis is in the upper reaches.

Peters Creek and Long Run

South of the city. Peters Creek runs from Piney Fork down past the Large Park and Ride. Long Run runs through Versailles Borough. Both are smaller and both fish better in the first month after stocking.

Butler and Beaver Counties

North and west, thirty to fifty minutes out.

  • Connoquenessing Creek from Lake Oneida to the SR 356 bridge. Bigger water with room to spread out.
  • Buffalo Creek from the upstream end of Chicora Borough Park downstream past Sarver Road. One of the better small-stream options in Butler County.
  • Slippery Rock Creek through McConnells Mill State Park. Big water, boulder gardens, and worth the trip for the scenery alone. Wade with care, because the gorge section is dangerous at high flows.
  • Brush Creek in Beaver County near Hazen was added to the trout stocking program for 2026, a 2.2 mile section from SR 588 downstream to the lower end of Brush Creek Park, stocked preseason and in-season.

Westmoreland County

Loyalhanna Creek

This is the Keystone Select water closest to Pittsburgh. Loyalhanna was one of the original eight streams in the program when it launched, and it remains one of the best big-trout destinations in western Pennsylvania.

Keystone Select sections receive large trout measuring 14 to 20 inches at a rate of 175 to 225 per mile, stocked during the spring stocking periods. The section runs under Delayed Harvest Artificial Lures Only regulations, which means artificial lures, flies, and streamers only, harvest limited to June 15 through Labor Day at three fish and a nine inch minimum, and zero harvest the rest of the year.

It also means the section is open year-round. In late February, when every standard stocked trout water in the state is closed, Loyalhanna is open. Our Keystone Select guide covers the program in detail.

The Loyalhanna gorge below Ligonier is the classic stretch. Route 30 parallels it, so access is straightforward and the pressure follows.

Somerset and Fayette Counties

The Laurel Highlands. Roughly 75 to 90 minutes from the city, and worth every minute in summer when the streams closer in are too warm.

Laurel Hill Creek

Another original Keystone Select stream. Laurel Hill runs through Somerset County and holds trout through the summer because of elevation and canopy. Laurel Hill State Park provides good access and a lake in the same trip.

Fayette County Mountain Water

Dunbar Creek, Indian Creek, and Meadow Run in Ohiopyle State Park all hold stocked trout, and all three run colder and later into the summer than the valley streams. Meadow Run in particular is worth a walk for the scenery even on a slow day.

These are small, technical streams with tight casting and clear water. Downsize everything.

Lawrence County

Neshannock Creek is the other original Keystone Select stream in the western half of the state, up near New Wilmington. Same program rules, same 14 to 20 inch fish, same Delayed Harvest structure.

Neshannock is about an hour north of Pittsburgh and gets meaningfully less traffic than Loyalhanna because it is farther from the metro. If you want the best odds on a Keystone Select fish without an opening-weekend crowd, this is the one.

Season by Season

Mid-February through opening day. Standard stocked waters close. The Delayed Harvest sections stay open, which in this region means Loyalhanna, Neshannock, and the Pine Creek section at North Park. Zero harvest, but you can fish.

Opening day through May. Everything is loaded. Fish anywhere. Expect company on North Park, Loyalhanna, and Slippery Rock.

June. The best month in this region. In-season stockings are still happening, the crowd is gone, and water temperatures have not turned yet. The Delayed Harvest harvest window also opens June 15.

July and August. Valley streams get too warm. Move to elevation. Laurel Hill Creek, the Fayette County mountain streams, and Meadow Run stay in range. Fish at first light.

October through mid-December. Fall stocking. Sixteen of the 24 Keystone Select sections receive a fall stocking, and lakes across the region get fish set up for ice. Our fall stocking guide covers what actually goes where.

What to Throw

On Standard Stocked Water

In the first 48 hours after a stocking, chartreuse or rainbow dough bait on a size 12 hook with split shot, or a single salmon egg drifted along the bottom seam. After the first week, switch to size 1 and 2 inline spinners. Gold blades in the stained water typical of western Pennsylvania after rain, silver on the rare clear day.

On Delayed Harvest and Keystone Select Sections

Bait is illegal, and so are molded imitations of worms, insects, and fish eggs. Legal and effective options are 1/16 and 1/32 ounce marabou jigs in olive, black, and white fished under a small float, size 1 inline spinners, and two inch countdown minnow baits.

On fly gear, beadhead pheasant tails and hare's ears in size 14 to 18, sucker spawn in white and pale yellow, and olive and black woolly buggers in size 8 and 10 for the larger carryover fish.

On the Mountain Streams

Small and light. Size 0 and 1 spinners, 4 pound line, and short accurate casts. On fly gear a three or four weight, size 14 to 18 dry flies in the summer, and a lot of walking.

Reading the Numbers Before You Drive

When you compare sections in our Pennsylvania data, fish per mile tells you more than the raw allocation count. A section with 1,800 planned trout across eight miles is thinner than a section with 700 across two.

Keep count and date separate. The count is the annual planned allocation for that section across the whole year. The scheduled date is when a truck is planned. Neither one guarantees the other, and all stockings are subject to change without notice.

Lakes Within an Hour

When the streams blow out, which happens often in western Pennsylvania, lakes save the day.

  • North Park Lake, Allegheny County. The closest stocked trout water to the city and the easiest bank access in the region.
  • Raccoon Lake in Raccoon Creek State Park, Beaver County. Good shoreline access and a large park around it.
  • Canonsburg Lake and Dutch Fork Lake, Washington County. Both PFBC-owned.
  • Glade Run Lake, Butler County.
  • Donegal Lake, Westmoreland County.
  • Green Lick Reservoir and Virgin Run Lake, Fayette County.
  • High Point Lake and Lake Somerset, Somerset County, both at elevation and both good for ice fishing.

Several of these receive November and December stockings that set up hardwater fishing in January and February. Our winter trout fishing guide covers how to work them once ice forms.

Western Pennsylvania Water Runs Different

Streams here carry color longer than eastern Pennsylvania water because of the clay and shale in the soil. A rain event that clears a Chester County creek in a day can leave a Butler County creek stained for three.

That changes lure selection. Gold and copper blades outfish silver here more often than they do in the east. Chartreuse and orange outfish natural colors. Bigger profiles get found in water where a subtle presentation disappears.

It also means the fishing stays good longer after a rain. Eastern anglers wait for water to clear. Western anglers fish the stain, because trout in colored water feed with more confidence and are far less leader-shy.

The other regional feature is acid mine drainage. Some watersheds in the coal counties carry legacy pollution that limits trout survival regardless of stocking. A section that gets fish every year and never seems to hold them may be running water the trout cannot live in past June. PFBC accounts for this in where it stocks, but it is worth understanding when you are choosing between two sections that look identical on paper.

The Youghiogheny Corridor

The Youghiogheny River through Ohiopyle State Park deserves a mention separate from the trout streams. It is primarily a smallmouth bass and whitewater river rather than stocked trout water, but the tributaries feeding it, including Meadow Run and the Fayette County mountain streams, hold trout and run cold.

A summer trip planned around the corridor gives you trout in the tributaries at first light, smallmouth in the river through the day, and a state park with camping in the middle of it. That is a better weekend than driving two hours each way for one species.

Quick Reference

  • Closest option. North Park Lake and Pine Creek, Allegheny County.
  • Best big fish. Loyalhanna Creek and Neshannock Creek, both Keystone Select.
  • Least crowded Keystone Select. Neshannock Creek, Lawrence County.
  • Best summer water. Laurel Hill Creek and the Fayette County mountain streams.
  • Best scenery. Slippery Rock Creek at McConnells Mill, and Meadow Run at Ohiopyle.
  • Open in late winter. The Delayed Harvest sections on Loyalhanna, Neshannock, and Pine Creek.

Stocking lists and section boundaries change annually. Verify your water against the current PFBC regulations summary before the drive.
