Keystone Select Stocked Trout Waters: The Complete Guide

Twenty-four stream sections, about 10,000 trout between 14 and 20 inches, and the rules that keep them in the water.

Fish Stocking Alert • Stocking Guide • 10 min read

Pennsylvania stocks roughly 3.2 million adult trout a year, and the average one measures 11 inches and weighs a little over half a pound. Keystone Select is the program that ignores that average entirely.

Under Keystone Select, the Fish and Boat Commission puts approximately 10,000 trout measuring 14 to 20 inches into 24 stream sections statewide. These are 2 and 3 year old fish, and they go in at a rate of 175 to 225 per mile. That density is comparable to what you find in Pennsylvania's best wild trout water. If you want a realistic shot at a stocked trout over 16 inches without driving to Erie, this is the program.

How the Program Works

Keystone Select started in 2018 with eight streams, one in each Commissioner district, chosen so no angler in the state was more than a few hours from one. Water had to be entirely open to public fishing to qualify. The program grew to 22 streams, then to the current 24 stream sections.

The original eight were Neshannock Creek, Loyalhanna Creek, Laurel Hill Creek, First Fork Sinnemahoning Creek, Wiconisco Creek, Loyalsock Creek, South Branch Tunkhannock Creek, and Middle Branch White Clay Creek. The 2017 expansion added Tulpehocken Creek, Chest Creek, Big Cove Creek, Harveys Creek, Kinzua Creek, and Oil Creek.

Twenty-three of the 24 sections are managed under Delayed Harvest Artificial Lures Only regulations. One section, Bald Eagle Creek Section 03 in Centre County within the Soaring Eagle Wetland, runs under a Miscellaneous Special Regulation that is identical to DHALO except that all tackle types are permitted. That is the only Keystone Select water where you can fish bait.

The Regulations You Are Fishing Under

Because almost every Keystone Select section is DHALO water, the rules are the DHALO rules.

  • Open year-round. No closed season. You can fish these sections in February when the rest of the stocked trout waters are shut.
  • Harvest window. June 15 through Labor Day only, three trout per day, nine inch minimum.
  • Zero harvest the rest of the year. From the day after Labor Day until June 15, the daily creel limit is zero.
  • Artificial lures only. Metal, plastic, rubber, or wood lures, plus flies and streamers. No natural bait, no baitfish, no fish eggs natural or molded, no other edible substance.
  • Spinning gear is fine. This is not a fly-only restriction. Lures may be fished on spinning or fly tackle.

Verify the current rules on the PFBC trout fishing regulations page before your first trip of the year. Section boundaries get revised, and waters occasionally get added or removed.

The Timing That Most Anglers Get Wrong

The big Keystone Select trout go in during the spring stocking periods only. PFBC schedules them that way to coincide with peak angler use around opening day.

Sixteen of the 24 sections also receive a fall stocking, limited to the sections PFBC considers destination fisheries with high fall traffic. Those fall stockings are standard adult trout, not another load of 18 inch fish.

The practical read: if you want the trophies, fish these sections in spring, or fish them in fall and winter for the carryover fish that survived the spring crowd. Zero harvest from September through mid-June means a lot of those fish are still in the section in November. They are also considerably harder to fool than they were in April, which is the fun part.

Fishing Them in Fall and Winter

Fall and winter on Keystone Select water is the underrated season. Nobody is removing fish, pressure drops off after Labor Day, and the trout have had months to spread into every piece of holding water in the section.

Carryover Keystone fish behave like wild trout by October. They sit in specific lies rather than schooling in the bridge pool, they feed on real forage, and they refuse anything dragged. Dead drift matters more than fly selection.

Small nymphs work best. Beadhead pheasant tails and hare's ears in size 14 to 18, olive and black woolly buggers in size 8 and 10, and small marabou jigs in 1/64 and 1/32 ounce under a float. On spinning gear, 1/16 ounce inline spinners with gold blades and small countdown minnow baits in the two inch range cover water fast.

Long leaders and light fluorocarbon matter in low winter water. Six pound is heavy for a January afternoon on a clear section.

Reading the Density Numbers

The 175 to 225 fish per mile rate is what makes Keystone Select different, and it is worth understanding how it compares to a normal stocked section.

A standard stocked stream section might get a few hundred adult trout spread across three or four miles. A Keystone Select section gets 175 to 225 large trout per mile of a section that is often only one to three miles long. Middle Branch White Clay Creek runs 1.67 miles. South Branch Tunkhannock Creek runs 0.99 mile. Harveys Creek runs 1.7 miles. That is a lot of big fish in a short stretch of water.

When you look at our Pennsylvania data, compare the fish-per-mile figure on a Keystone Select section against the neighboring standard sections. The gap is the whole argument for driving there. Keep in mind that our counts are annual planned allocations for the section across the full year, not per-event actuals, and the scheduled date is a separate fact from the count.

Notes on Specific Sections

Tulpehocken Creek, Berks County

The 3.8 mile Keystone Select section runs from the first deflector below Blue Marsh Dam downstream to the T-921 covered bridge. This is a tailwater, which means cold releases keep it fishable in summer when other stocked water is too warm. Closest Keystone Select water to Philadelphia and Reading, and the pressure reflects it. Immediately downstream of the covered bridge the water switches to the year-round program, which is worth knowing when you plan a day.

Middle Branch White Clay Creek, Chester County

A 1.67 mile section from Good Hope Road downstream to the confluence with the East Branch. This is the southeast corner option and the closest big-trout water to Philadelphia's western suburbs.

Harveys Creek and South Branch Tunkhannock Creek

Harveys Creek in Luzerne County runs 1.7 miles from Jackson Road to Pavlick Road. South Branch Tunkhannock Creek runs 0.99 mile from SR 6 through the Keystone College campus in Lackawanna and Wyoming counties. Both are short, both are accessible, and both concentrate fish heavily.

McMichael Creek, Monroe County

A 2.42 mile section in the heart of the Poconos, running from below the Turkey Hill Road bridge downstream to the Glenbrook Road bridge. Convenient if you are already up there for a weekend. Our Pocono trout fishing guide covers the rest of that corner.

Bald Eagle Creek Section 03, Centre County

The one Keystone Select water that permits all tackle types, located within the Soaring Eagle Wetland in Huston Township. Same harvest window and same nine inch minimum as DHALO, but you can fish bait. The section has accessible parking and a trail to a fishing platform, which makes it one of the better options in the state for anglers with mobility limitations.

Access and Pressure

Every Keystone Select water is fully open to public fishing. That was a program requirement from the start. What varies is parking and the length of the walk.

The short sections are the crowded ones on spring weekends. If you are fishing Tulpehocken or Middle Branch White Clay in April, plan on company. Weekday mornings and the September through December window are a completely different experience on the same water.

How This Fits With the Rest of the State

Keystone Select is one lane in a larger special-regulation system. Delayed Harvest sections that are not part of Keystone Select still get standard stocked trout under the same year-round, zero-harvest-in-winter structure, and there are far more of them. Our guide to Pennsylvania delayed harvest waters covers the full program.

If you want big stocked trout outside the Keystone Select sections, the other route is the trophy brood fish PFBC distributes across regular stocked waters. Our guide to Pennsylvania's biggest stocked trout covers where those go.

What a Carryover Keystone Fish Is Like

The program stocks 14 to 20 inch trout, and a share of them survive a full year under zero-harvest rules from September through mid-June. Those survivors change.

A hatchery rainbow that spends twelve months eating sculpins, crayfish, and drifting nymphs instead of pellets puts on real weight and loses the soft, pale look. Fins regenerate. Coloring darkens. By the second spring, a carryover Keystone Select brown is functionally a wild-behaving fish that happens to have come from a truck.

It also gets much harder to catch. Twelve months of catch and release pressure in a one to three mile section means every one of those fish has been hooked, or has watched something else get hooked. They refuse drag. They sit tight to structure. They feed hardest in the last hour of light.

This is why regulars on these sections stop caring about the stocking calendar. The fish that matter to them went in a year ago.

Weekday Fishing Changes the Program Completely

Keystone Select sections are short, well publicized, and easy to find. On an April Saturday, a 1.67 mile section can hold thirty anglers.

The same section on a Wednesday in late April holds two. Same fish, same water, a completely different day. If your schedule allows a weekday at all, spend it here rather than on a standard stocked stream where the crowd matters less.

The other low-pressure window is the stretch from late September through February. Harvest is closed, the season feels over to most anglers, and the fish density is at its annual peak.

Fishing Pressure and Fly Choice

On heavily pressured Keystone Select water, the standard patterns stop producing by mid-May. Anglers who keep catching fish do two things.

They go smaller than seems reasonable. Size 18 to 22 zebra midges, small pheasant tails, and 6X fluorocarbon in low clear water. A section that refuses a size 14 nymph will often take a size 20 without hesitation.

They fish the water between the obvious spots. Everyone works the head of the pool and the deep slot. The four foot pocket behind a mid-stream boulder, the six inches of soft water against an undercut bank, and the tailout nobody bothers with all hold fish that have not been shown a fly that day.

Quick Reference

  • Program size. 24 stream sections, approximately 10,000 trout at 14 to 20 inches.
  • Density. 175 to 225 large trout per mile.
  • Fish age. Two to three years old.
  • Regulations. DHALO on 23 sections. Bald Eagle Creek Section 03 in Centre County is Miscellaneous Special Regulation, all tackle permitted.
  • Harvest. June 15 through Labor Day, three per day, nine inch minimum. Zero the rest of the year.
  • Big fish timing. Spring stockings only. Sixteen sections also get a standard fall stocking.
  • Best low-pressure window. October through February, fishing carryover trout.
