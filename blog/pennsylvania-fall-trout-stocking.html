Fishing Pennsylvania Stocked Trout in Fall

Pennsylvania stocks trout into October, November, and December, and you can fish them the same day the truck leaves.

Fish Stocking Alert • Stocking Guide • 10 min read

Most Pennsylvania trout anglers treat the season as a spring event. They fish hard from opening day through Memorial Day, the water warms up, and the rods go in the garage until next March. That leaves the best stocked trout fishing of the year to a small crowd that knows the fall schedule.

Pennsylvania stocks adult trout from early October into mid-December. Water temperatures are in the range trout actually want, the fish are fresh, and the parking lots that were full in April are empty. Here is how the fall program works and how to fish it.

The Fall Stocking Window Runs October Into Mid-December

Fall stocking in Pennsylvania begins in early October and continues through the middle of December. It is a much smaller program than the spring effort, and it is targeted rather than statewide.

The 2025 season gives a good sense of the scale. The Fish and Boat Commission stocked roughly 116,500 adult rainbow, brown, and brook trout into 118 stream sections and lakes between October 1 and mid-December. October carried most of it, with about 89,000 trout going into 93 waters, split between 53 lakes and 40 stream sections. Another 25 lakes received approximately 28,600 trout across November and December, largely to set up ice fishing.

Compare that to the spring program, where the PFBC stocks approximately 3.2 million adult trout into 684 streams and 130 lakes. Fall is a fraction of the annual total. The tradeoff is that fall fish land in a much smaller number of waters and face a fraction of the angling pressure.

Two things to keep straight when you read any stocking data, ours included. Planned allocation counts and scheduled dates are separate facts. A section listed with a planned allocation of 1,200 rainbow trout for the year and a stocking scheduled the week of October 12 is not a section that receives 1,200 trout on October 12. The count is the annual plan across all stockings. The date is when a truck is scheduled to show up. All stockings are subject to change without notice.

Fall-Stocked Trout Can Be Fished the Same Day

This is the rule that makes fall worth planning around. There is no closed period on fall stockings. Trout stocked in October, November, and December can be fished for immediately.

Compare that to the preseason period. Stocked trout waters close to all fishing from mid-February until 8 a.m. on the opening day of trout season, with the Mentored Youth Trout Day as the only exception. Anglers spend six weeks watching trucks dump fish into water they are not allowed to touch.

In fall, the truck leaves and you can start casting. If you know the date, you can be there.

The Extended Season Cuts Your Daily Limit

Pennsylvania runs two limit structures on stocked trout waters. During the regular season, from the opening day of trout season through Labor Day, the daily limit is five trout of combined species with a seven inch minimum.

The extended season starts the day after Labor Day and runs into the following February. For the current cycle, that extended season began September 8, 2026, and runs through February 15, 2027. During the extended season, the daily limit drops to three trout of combined species, still with a seven inch minimum.

So every fall-stocked trout you catch falls under the three fish limit, not five. Special regulation waters have their own rules that override these numbers, which matters more in fall than at any other time of year. Verify current limits on the PFBC trout fishing regulations page before you keep anything.

Lakes Get Most of the Fall Fish

The fall program leans heavily toward lakes and impoundments. In the 2025 October round, 53 of the 93 waters stocked were lakes. The November and December round was lakes only, all 25 of them.

The reason is ice. Those late-season lake stockings are deliberately timed so that fish are in place and settled when hardwater conditions arrive in January and February. A lake stocked the second week of December is an ice fishing plan, not a December bank fishing plan, though it fishes fine as both.

The practical consequence is that if you are a stream angler, your fall options are narrower than your spring options. Roughly 40 stream sections statewide received October fish in 2025 out of 684 streams stocked over the full year. Knowing which ones is most of the game.

Keystone Select Waters in Fall

Sixteen of the 24 Keystone Select Stocked Trout Water sections receive a fall stocking. The PFBC limits fall stocking on these waters to sections it considers destination fisheries with high fall angler use.

Set expectations correctly on what shows up. The large Keystone Select fish, the 2 to 3 year old trout measuring 14 to 20 inches that the program is famous for, are stocked only during the spring stocking periods to line up with peak angler use. The fall stocking on a Keystone Select section is standard adult trout, averaging around 11 inches, not another load of trophies.

That does not make it a bad plan. It makes it a different plan. You are fishing a stretch of water that already holds carryover Keystone fish from spring under catch and release rules, with a fresh load of average trout added on top. Some of those spring-stocked 18 inch fish are still in there in October, and the ones that survived six months of pressure are considerably smarter than they were in April.

Delayed Harvest Water Is Catch and Release All Fall

Delayed Harvest Artificial Lures Only sections are open to fishing year-round with no closed season, which already makes them a fall option. The limit structure is what defines the fall experience there.

On DHALO water, harvest is allowed from June 15 through Labor Day with a nine inch minimum and a three fish daily limit. From the day after Labor Day until June 15, the daily creel limit is zero. Every fall and winter trout you catch on a DHALO section goes back.

Gear is restricted to artificial lures built from metal, plastic, rubber, or wood, plus flies and streamers. No natural bait, no baitfish, no fish eggs, natural or molded, and no other edible substance. Lures can be fished on spinning or fly gear, so this is not a fly-only restriction.

The upside of a zero-harvest fall is fish density. Nobody is removing trout from these sections from September through the following June, so the population compounds. DHALO water in November holds more trout than almost anything else in the state.

Fall Trout Behave Differently Than April Trout

A trout stocked in April lands in water that is often in the 40s and warming into a hatch cycle. A trout stocked in October lands in water in the 50s and cooling. That difference shows up in where the fish go and what they eat.

Fall-stocked trout disperse faster. Spring fish tend to pile up near the stocking access point for days, which is why the crowds work the bridge pool. Fall fish spread out through a section within a day or two, partly because the water is comfortable everywhere and partly because there is far less pressure pushing them around.

Fall fish also feed more consistently through the middle of the day. In April you are often waiting for water to warm past a threshold before anything happens. In late October and November, a 45 degree morning that climbs to 55 by noon puts fish on the feed for a long midday window.

Leaf litter is the fall complication. After the first serious leaf drop, every drift picks up debris and pools fill in. Fish move to water with current strong enough to keep itself clean, which concentrates them in ways that work in your favor once you spot the pattern.

Where Fall-Stocked Trout Hold

  • Tailouts of pools. The lower third of a pool where it starts to shallow and pick up speed. Fall trout sit here more than they do in spring.
  • Seams beside fast water. The soft edge where a riffle dumps into a pool. Fish face the current and let food come to them.
  • Undercut banks and log jams. With leaves down and water clearer, overhead cover matters more.
  • Deep lake points and creek channels. On stocked lakes, fall fish relate to the first significant depth change off the stocking access point.
  • Anywhere the leaves are not. Find the current lanes that stay clean and you find the fish.

What to Throw

On Streams

Inline spinners in size 1 and 2 are the most efficient search tool for fresh fall fish. Gold blades in stained water, silver in clear. Small spoons in the 1/8 to 1/6 ounce range fished on a slow retrieve get bigger carryover fish that ignore spinners.

Where bait is legal, a single salmon egg or a small piece of nightcrawler drifted on a size 10 or 12 hook under enough split shot to tick bottom is still the most productive presentation on freshly stocked trout. Fluorescent orange and chartreuse dough baits work in the first week after a stocking, then lose effectiveness as the fish switch to natural forage.

On DHALO and other artificial-only water, run olive and black woolly buggers in size 8 and 10, plus beadhead pheasant tails and hare's ear nymphs in size 14 to 18. Small marabou jigs in 1/64 and 1/32 ounce under a float cover cold-water fish well.

On Lakes

PowerBait floated off a slip-sinker rig with an 18 to 24 inch leader is the fall lake standard, in chartreuse and rainbow colors. Cast toward the first depth break rather than straight out from the bank.

For anglers who would rather move, small spoons in 1/8 ounce and countdown minnow baits in the two inch range fished along points cover water faster and select for larger fish. Timing Your Trip Around a Stocking

The first 48 hours after a fall stocking produce the highest catch rates and the least selective fish. Days three through seven produce fewer fish but better quality, because the trout have dispersed and the crowd has moved on.

Weather matters more in fall than spring. A cold front dropping water temperature five degrees overnight shuts down a stocked section that was excellent the day before. Rain is generally good until it blows the creek out. The same principles in our guide to reading water conditions before you fish apply directly here.

How to Find This Year's Fall List

The PFBC typically announces the fall and winter stocking plan in late September, ahead of the October 1 start. That announcement includes the number of waters, the total fish, and the schedule. Between announcements, the PFBC trout stocking page is the authoritative source.

Schedules shift. Water temperature swings and hatchery logistics move stockings around, sometimes by a week or more. Check close to your trip rather than relying on a date you wrote down in September. Our guide to reading the Pennsylvania trout stocking schedule covers how to work a section list into a plan.

Quick Reference

  • Window. Early October through mid-December, with the bulk of it in October.
  • Closed period. None. Fall-stocked trout can be fished the same day they go in.
  • Daily limit. Three trout combined species, seven inch minimum, during the extended season running September 8, 2026 through February 15, 2027.
  • Water mix. Weighted toward lakes, especially the November and December stockings.
  • Keystone Select. Sixteen of 24 sections get fall fish, but the 14 to 20 inch trophies go in during spring only.
  • Delayed Harvest. Zero harvest from the day after Labor Day until June 15. Artificial lures only.
  • Best conditions. Stable weather, water in the 45 to 58 degree range, within a week of a stocking date.
