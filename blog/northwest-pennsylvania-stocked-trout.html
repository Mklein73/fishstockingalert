Northwest Pennsylvania Stocked Trout Waters

Everyone drives here for steelhead, which leaves the inland stocked program to the anglers who bothered to look it up.

Fish Stocking Alert • Location Guide • 10 min read

Say northwest Pennsylvania to most anglers and they think steelhead. That is fair, and the Erie tributaries deserve the attention, but the region carries a full inland stocked trout program that runs on a completely different calendar and gets a fraction of the traffic.

Erie, Crawford, Mercer, Venango, Warren, Butler, and Lawrence counties hold two Keystone Select streams, multiple Delayed Harvest sections, and a long list of stocked streams and lakes. This guide covers the inland water. For the tributary run, see our Lake Erie steelhead guide.

The Two Keystone Select Streams

Neshannock Creek, Lawrence County

One of the original eight Keystone Select streams from the program's 2018 launch, running near New Wilmington. Under Keystone Select, PFBC stocks trout measuring 14 to 20 inches at a rate of 175 to 225 fish per mile, during the spring stocking periods.

The section runs under Delayed Harvest Artificial Lures Only regulations. Artificial lures, flies, and streamers only. Harvest allowed June 15 through Labor Day at three fish and a nine inch minimum. Zero harvest the rest of the year. Open year-round.

Neshannock is about an hour north of Pittsburgh, which puts it far enough from the metro to stay reasonable on weekends.

Oil Creek, Venango and Crawford Counties

Added to Keystone Select in the 2017 expansion. Oil Creek runs through Oil Creek State Park between Titusville and Oil City, with a bike trail paralleling much of it and good public access throughout.

The park also holds separate Delayed Harvest areas at Petroleum Centre, running about 1.6 miles from the PennDOT bridge downstream to the OC&T Railroad bridge at Columbia Farm, and near the Drake Well Museum. Same DHALO rules apply on those sections.

Our Keystone Select guide covers the program in full.

Erie County Inland

Separate from the tributaries, Erie County holds inland stocked water. Elk Creek appears in the regional stocked trout listings in stretches around the I-79 and SR 98 crossings and above Halls Run, and it also functions as a steelhead tributary depending on the section and season.

One rule to keep straight. Inland ponds, lakes, and reservoirs in Erie County do not require a Lake Erie Permit. The tributaries do, in their entirety, along with everything flowing into them. If you are fishing an inland Erie County lake, your standard license and trout permit cover you.

Nursery Waters in Erie County are closed to all fishing, wading, and even possession of fishing equipment while in or along them. Those are Trout Run and its tributaries, Godfrey Run, Orchard Beach Run, and Crooked Creek where posted. Know them before you park.

Crawford County

The lake county. Crawford holds more still water than any other county in the region, and several of those lakes receive trout.

  • Tamarack Lake, a PFBC-owned lake near Meadville.
  • Woodcock Creek Lake, an Army Corps impoundment north of Meadville.
  • Sugar Lake and Conneaut Lake, the largest natural lake in Pennsylvania.
  • Pymatuning Reservoir on the Ohio line, which runs under its own set of regulations separate from standard inland rules.
  • French Creek, stocked from the Pennsylvania and New York line down to the West Branch. French Creek is one of the most biologically diverse waterways in the northeast, and the trout are a small part of what lives there.

Venango, Mercer, and Butler

  • Little Sandy Creek, stocked from above the Bombishi Road bridge to the old bridge at the Polk Center pump house.
  • Little Scrubgrass Creek in Venango County.
  • North Deer Creek in Mercer County had its stocking limits revised for 2026 and now extends 6.06 miles from the Bortz and Lower Road bridge downstream to the mouth.
  • Kahle Lake and Justus Lake at Two Mile Run County Park for still water in Venango County.
  • Connoquenessing Creek in Butler County, from Lake Oneida to the SR 356 bridge.
  • Buffalo Creek in Butler County, from the upstream end of Chicora Borough Park downstream past Sarver Road.
  • Bear Creek, stocked from above the Troutman crossing to the first bridge above Bruin.
  • Slippery Rock Creek through McConnells Mill State Park on the Butler and Lawrence line. Big, boulder-strewn, scenic, and hazardous at high water.

Warren and McKean

The Allegheny National Forest corner. Kinzua Creek in McKean County is a Keystone Select stream from the 2017 expansion. Its Delayed Harvest section boundaries have been revised in recent years, so verify the current limits before you fish it.

This corner of the state has the most public land and the fewest anglers. If solitude ranks above fish count for you, this is where to go.

The Two-Season Advantage

Northwest Pennsylvania is the only region in the state where you can plan a weekend around two separate trout fisheries running at the same time.

From October through April, steelhead are in the Erie tributaries. From October into mid-December, PFBC runs its fall stocking program on inland waters. Those windows overlap almost exactly.

The practical value shows up when the weather does not cooperate. Erie tributaries are flashy. A one inch rain takes them from a trickle to unfishable mud in hours. If you drove four hours for steelhead and the creeks are chocolate, an inland stocked stream or a lake that took fish the week before saves the trip.

Plan it in reverse too. If the creeks are low and clear and the steelhead are spooky, spend the morning on inland water and hit the tributaries at last light.

Timing

Mid-February to opening day. Standard stocked waters close. Keystone Select and Delayed Harvest sections on Neshannock, Oil Creek, and Kinzua stay open, zero harvest. Steelhead are still in the tributaries.

April and May. Preseason and in-season stockings load the region. Spring steelhead and dropback fish overlap with opening day, which is one of the best weeks of the year to be in Erie County.

June. The strongest inland month. Stockings continue, crowds thin, and the Delayed Harvest harvest window opens June 15.

July and August. Most northwest freestone streams run warm. Shift to the lakes and to the deeper, shaded sections. Early morning only.

October through mid-December. Fall stocking on inland waters plus the peak of the steelhead run. Best weeks of the year in this region. Our fall stocking guide covers what goes where.

What to Throw

Inland Streams

Northwest Pennsylvania water carries color after rain more persistently than the eastern half of the state because of the clay and shale soils. Gold blades on size 1 and 2 inline spinners are the default. Where bait is legal in the first week after a stocking, chartreuse dough bait or a single salmon egg on a size 12 hook.

Keystone Select and Delayed Harvest Sections

Artificial lures, flies, and streamers only. Marabou jigs in 1/32 and 1/16 ounce under a small float in olive, black, and white. Size 1 inline spinners. Two inch countdown minnow baits. On fly gear, beadhead pheasant tails in size 14 to 18, sucker spawn, and olive woolly buggers in size 8 and 10.

Lakes

Slip sinker rig with an 18 to 24 inch fluorocarbon leader and floating dough bait in chartreuse or rainbow. Cast to the first depth break rather than straight out. For better fish, small spoons in 1/8 ounce and countdown minnow baits worked along points.

Access

Public access is a strength here. Oil Creek State Park, McConnells Mill State Park, the Allegheny National Forest, Pymatuning State Park, and the State Game Lands parcels scattered across Crawford, Venango, and Warren counties provide reliable parking and walk-in water.

Private posting is less of a problem than in the southeast, but it exists. Check current access before you plan a specific stretch.

Pymatuning and the Big Water

Pymatuning Reservoir on the Ohio line is the largest lake in the region at roughly 17,000 acres, and it runs under its own set of regulations separate from standard inland rules. Check the Pymatuning-specific section of the regulations summary before you fish it, because the seasons and limits there differ from the rest of the state.

Pymatuning is primarily a walleye, muskellunge, crappie, and perch fishery rather than a trout destination, which makes it the right answer when the trout streams are blown out or too warm. Conneaut Lake, the largest natural lake in Pennsylvania, sits nearby and fishes similarly.

The practical value for a trout angler is flexibility. A weekend in Crawford County can shift from stocked trout to walleye to steelhead depending on what the weather does, and all three are inside a 45 minute drive of Meadville.

Acid Mine Drainage and What It Means

Parts of Venango, Clarion, and Jefferson counties carry legacy pollution from coal and oil extraction. Some tributaries in these watersheds run acidic enough that trout cannot survive in them past the first few weeks after stocking.

PFBC accounts for this in where it stocks, so the sections on the schedule are generally viable. What it means for you is that a section that gets fish every year and never seems to hold them past May may be limited by water chemistry rather than by angling pressure.

Restoration work by watershed associations has improved several of these systems substantially over the past two decades. Oil Creek is a good example of a stream that recovered enough to support a Keystone Select designation.

The Allegheny National Forest

Warren, McKean, Elk, and Forest counties hold over half a million acres of national forest, and it is the most underfished trout country in Pennsylvania.

Access is close to unlimited. Forest roads reach most drainages, dispersed camping is permitted in much of it, and there is no posting to navigate. The tradeoff is that the streams are small, the road network is confusing, and cell service is unreliable.

Kinzua Creek is the marquee water because of its Keystone Select designation. The smaller tributaries throughout the forest hold wild brook trout under their own regulations, which differ from stocked trout rules.

Bring paper maps, tell someone where you are going, and plan on a full day rather than an evening trip.

Weather Is the Real Variable

Lake Erie drives the weather in this corner of the state in ways that matter to a fishing plan.

Lake effect snow in Erie and Crawford counties routinely buries roads that are clear thirty miles inland. In November and December, a forecast that looks fine in Pittsburgh can mean two feet on the ground in North East. Check conditions along your route rather than at the destination.

The lake also moderates fall temperatures near the shore, which extends the fishable season on inland waters in Erie County by a couple of weeks compared to inland counties at the same latitude.

In spring, the lake works the opposite way. Erie County runs colder and later than the rest of the region, so the inland stocked streams there fish well into June after other water has warmed.

Quick Reference

  • Best big fish. Neshannock Creek and Oil Creek, both Keystone Select.
  • Best public access. Oil Creek State Park, with a bike trail along the water.
  • Best lakes. Tamarack, Woodcock Creek, Sugar Lake, Conneaut Lake, Justus Lake.
  • Best solitude. Warren and McKean counties in the Allegheny National Forest.
  • Best combined trip. October and November, inland fall stockings plus the steelhead run.
  • Permit note. Inland Erie County lakes do not require a Lake Erie Permit. Tributaries do, in their entirety.
  • Closed water. Trout Run, Godfrey Run, Orchard Beach Run, and Crooked Creek where posted are Nursery Waters.

Section boundaries and stocking lists change annually. Verify against the current PFBC regulations summary before the drive.
